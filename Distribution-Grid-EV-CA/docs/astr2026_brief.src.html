6EV charging energy is about doubleTo do

The charging input simulates 2.5 million vehicles at 2025 adoption. It gives each vehicle the daily car mileage of a whole household, 79 miles on average, where its settings call for 35 miles per vehicle. Charging energy is therefore about twice the intended level: 19.8 against 10.6 TWh a year. Every substation also receives the same hourly profile. The EV increment in point 3 inherits both.

To doAssign per-vehicle mileage and zone-specific charging profiles, then solve again.

7Network data predate recent solar and storage interconnections

Outside PG&E, line geometry dates from 2008–2010. Collector substations and tie lines of plants commissioned since are absent, so these plants attach to the nearest legacy substation, a median 6 km away. At {{n:n_over}} substations attached capacity exceeds outgoing corridor capacity by {{n:over_gw}} GW, and at least {{n:spill_over}} of the {{n:spill}} GWh of curtailment arises there. Corridor relief releases it, overstating the CO2 saving in point 5.

DecisionHow to obtain the missing facilities (CAISO or utility data, interconnection records, OpenStreetMap), and what fallback is acceptable (a tie line sized to the plant)?

8Data needed

  • Line thermal ratings, normal and emergency. None is public.
  • SDG&E substation load. It settles point 4.
  • Lines and collector substations built since 2010 in SCE and SDG&E (point 7).
  • Transformer bank ratings where derived, mainly SCE and SDG&E.
  • SDG&E feeder map, to route EV load as in PG&E and SCE.
  • EV adoption projections beyond 2025.
