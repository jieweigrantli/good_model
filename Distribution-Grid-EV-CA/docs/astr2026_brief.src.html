Storage against transmission for EV-driven congestion

Where the model stands, what it shows, and the decisions it needs.

Jiewei Grant Li · UC Davis · results from the re-run of 6 October, four seasonal weeks, 2023 generator fleet

1The model runs

PG&E, SCE and SDG&E are modelled substation by substation: {{n:substations}} substations and {{n:corridors}} corridors, rebuilt from public line data. The network sits inside a balancing-area model of the Western grid and is dispatched as one linear program over four seasonal weeks, with the grid as built and the generator fleet that existed in 2023. Eight scenarios solve in about 90 minutes.

6EV charging energy is about double

The input is 2.5 million vehicles at 2025 adoption. Each carries its household's daily mileage, 79 miles where 35 was intended, so the fleet draws 19.8 TWh a year where about 10.6 was intended. Every substation also gets the same hourly charging shape. The EV increment in point 3 inherits both.

DecisionCorrect the mileage, and which adoption years to run? Inputs stop at 2025.

7SCE and SDG&E lines date from 2010

The line layer for SCE and SDG&E was drawn in 2008 to 2010. Solar and storage built since land on the nearest old substation, a median 6 km away. At {{n:n_over}} substations plants exceed the lines leaving by {{n:over_gw}} GW and the model throws the output away: at least {{n:spill_over}} of the {{n:spill}} GWh spilled. The corridor CO2 saving in point 5 comes from releasing spill, so it is overstated.

DecisionPatch each plant with a tie line, or rebuild both utilities from OpenStreetMap, which has the missing lines?
